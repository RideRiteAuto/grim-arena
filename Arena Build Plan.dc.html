The one thing to get right

Combat is real-time and physical, not tick-based. Free WASD movement, mouse-aimed swings, dodge rolls, shield raises, projectiles with travel time. Every hit is a swept-capsule check against an animation window — never a dice roll on a timer.

A note on IP

Mechanics are free to borrow. Names and art are not. This plan uses original item names and original assets throughout so you can share the link with anyone without worry.

02

Netcode

Two players over a home tunnel means 40–120ms of latency. Action combat feels broken at that ping unless you handle it deliberately. Three techniques, in order of importance:

i

Client-side prediction

Your own character moves the instant you press a key. Every input is stamped with a sequence number and kept in a ring buffer. When the server's snapshot arrives it carries the last input it processed — if its position differs from your recorded one, snap and replay all unacknowledged inputs. Movement feels local at any ping.

ii

Entity interpolation

Your opponent is rendered 100ms in the past, smoothly interpolated between the last two snapshots. Never extrapolate a player — a rubber-banding enemy is worse than a slightly delayed one.

iii

Lag-compensated hits

The server keeps one second of positional history per player. When a swing lands, it rewinds the target to where the attacker actually saw them (their render time = now − RTT/2 − interp delay) and tests the hitbox there. Without this, hits visibly pass through people.

The block check runs on the rewound state too — otherwise raising your shield feels late.

Cheating is not your problem. You're playing with one coworker. Keep the server authoritative because it makes the game feel consistent, not because you need anti-cheat. Don't spend a day on input validation.

Combat design

Every action is a state machine with explicit frame windows: windup → active → recovery. You are committed during windup and active; you can cancel into a dodge only during recovery. This is what makes action combat readable instead of mushy.

Blade & Shield

Light attacks chain into a 3-hit combo with a widening arc. Hold right-mouse to raise the shield: 80% reduction in a 120° frontal cone, movement cut to 40%, drains a stamina bar. Release-on-impact within 200ms of a hit is a perfect block — zero damage and the attacker is staggered for 0.5s. That single mechanic carries the whole melee skill ceiling.

Frost Staff

Frostbolt is a slow, visible projectile — 18 m/s, leads badly, punished by strafing. On hit: damage plus 2s root. Rooted players can still turn, attack and block; they cannot walk or dodge. A 6s cooldown on the freeze specifically, so you can't perma-lock. Mana regenerates only when you're not casting.

Longbow

Infinite arrows, two fire modes. Charged: hold to draw up to 1.2s, damage and projectile speed scale with draw, movement slowed while drawing. Rapid: three fast arrows in 0.9s, low damage, generous spread, no charge. Shield cone blocks arrows, so ranged wants angles.

Weapon switching & the triangle

Keys 1/2/3 switch loadout with a 0.6s lockout — long enough that switching is a decision, short enough to be part of a combo. Keep a soft rock-paper-scissors: magic pierces the shield cone at 50% reduction, arrows are fully blockable, melee beats a caster who can't kite. Don't hard-counter anything; multipliers of 1.25× / 1.0× / 0.8× are plenty.

Hitsplats & feedback

Chunky opaque plates with a bold centered numeral, projected from world space as DOM elements — cheaper and crisper than sprites, and trivially styleable. Original shape language (a hard-edged beveled hex rather than the classic teardrop blob), same readability rules that make the genre's splats work.

Fully opaque fill, hard 2px dark outline — legible on any background
Numeral centered and optically balanced, never clipped

Pair every splat with 60ms of hitstop, a 6px camera shake, a red vignette pulse when you're the one hit, and a sound. Feedback is 80% of how good combat feels — budget real time for it, not the last afternoon.

04

Arena & art direction

A sunken stone octagon, roughly 30m across, walled in weathered blocks with braziers at the corners and two opposing entry arches. Sand floor, a few broken pillars for cover so the ranged fight isn't a flat plane.

Looking good, cheaply

Stylized low-poly, hand-authored colours
Baked lightmaps on all static geometry
One directional light + hemisphere fill
Contact-hardening shadows on players only
Fog for depth and to hide the arena edge
Post: bloom on braziers, vignette, subtle grain
Trim-sheet texturing — one 1024 atlas, one draw call

Performance budget

60fps on integrated graphics, non-negotiable
<120 draw calls, <80k triangles total
Instanced pillars, braziers and rubble
GPU particles for frost, embers, dust
<8MB initial download so the link loads fast
Cap devicePixelRatio at 1.5
Preload everything behind a loading gate

Characters

Start with capsule-and-cone placeholders and ship the whole combat system against them — you'll iterate ten times faster. Then swap in Quaternius CC0 knights (free, low-poly, game-ready) with Mixamo animations retargeted onto them: idle, run, strafe, three sword swings, block, block-impact, cast, draw-bow, release, hit-react, death. Weapons attach to a hand bone so switching is just visibility toggles. One GLB, one skeleton, animations packed together.

05

Hosting & the link

You want to run it locally and send a URL. Cloudflare Tunnel is the right answer: no router config, no account needed for a quick throwaway link, and it terminates HTTPS for you — which matters because a secure page can only open a secure WebSocket.

TERMINAL
# run the game node server/index.js → listening on :8080 # expose it, in a second terminal cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8080 → https://amber-quiet-fox.trycloudflare.com # send that URL. that's it.

Gotchas

Derive the socket URL from location, never hardcode localhost
Use wss:// when the page is https
Quick tunnels get a new URL each restart
Your machine sleeping kills the game

When you outgrow it

The same Node process deploys unchanged to Fly.io or Railway for a few dollars a month, and you get a stable URL, better routing and no dependency on your laptop being awake. Worth doing once other people want to play.

Scope discipline

IN THE MVP
Two players, one arena, room codes
All three combat styles, hotkey switching
Blocking, perfect block, dodge roll
Hitsplats, health bars, hitstop, screen shake
Best of three rounds with a reset
Sound effects and a music bed
EXPLICITLY LATER
Accounts, persistence, progression
Inventory, loot, crafting, food
More than two players or teams
Multiple arenas, PvE, mobile support
Voice chat, spectators, replays
Anti-cheat beyond server authority

The failure mode for a project like this is building a lobby system and an inventory before the sword ever connects with someone. Get two capsules hitting each other over a real socket in week one — everything after that is polish on a thing that already works.
